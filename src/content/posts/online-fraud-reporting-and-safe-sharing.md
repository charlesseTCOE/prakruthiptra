An urgent message can make an unfamiliar payment link look convincing. If a message claims to come from a service provider or the association, confirm it through a contact you already trust before acting.

## If you suspect financial cyber fraud

I4C’s CyberDost advisory asks people to report financial cyber fraud promptly on **1930**. Contact your bank through its official app, website or the number on your card, and report the incident at the [National Cybercrime Reporting Portal](https://cybercrime.gov.in/).

Save transaction references, relevant messages and screenshots for the reporting process. Do not post them unredacted in community groups. Reporting enables authorities to act; it does not guarantee recovery of money.

## Share the warning, not your private records

When warning neighbours, describe the type of message and how you checked it. Remove account numbers, identity documents and unrelated personal details. Do not forward a clickable suspicious link when a plain description would do.

Use the [official PTRA channels](/#channels) listed on this website. A familiar display name alone is not proof of who sent a message.
